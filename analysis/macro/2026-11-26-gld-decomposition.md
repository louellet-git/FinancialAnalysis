# GLD: What Its Return Is Made Of

*The quarterly return of the SPDR Gold Shares fund (GLD), October 2023 to September 2026, split into gold and fee. Layers 1 and 2 of 3.*

You asked: is GLD a black box? This page checks two things. First: does the report of the trust add up, and does the price change with the value of its gold (the net asset value, NAV)? Second: what made the return of each quarter? The answer: GLD is not a black box. Each quarter, the NAV return is the gold price return less the fee, to within 0.015 percentage points (pp).

## The answer

1. **The trust adds up.** In each of its 13 quarterly and yearly reports to the SEC (10-Q and 10-K), the sums agree with its NAV per share to the cent, after the gold of unsettled creations and redemptions. This is a check of the arithmetic of the report, not of the gold price.
2. **Gold and the fee explain the return.** In each of 12 quarters, the NAV return is the gold price return plus the fee (-0.093 to -0.103 pp each quarter). The rest is at most 0.015 pp (Table 1). Over the 3 years, gold rose 123% and the NAV 121%. The difference is the fee: the gold for each share fell by 1.2%.
3. **The price follows the NAV.** At each quarter end, the GLD close is within -1.2% and +1.6% of the NAV. Each gap is smaller than the range of the GLD price on that day. The gap is consistent with timing: the NAV uses the London gold price (about 15:00 London), and the close is at 16:00 New York.

**What this means for you.** This test shows no hidden part: the reports read as gold, less 0.40% a year. So the question "why did GLD move?" is the question "why did gold move?". Layer 3 tests that. Page 2 gives the limits.
