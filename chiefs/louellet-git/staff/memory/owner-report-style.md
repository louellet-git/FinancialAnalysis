# What the person wants in a report (his review of the S&P 10 page, 2026-11-21)

One page of the Head of Staff. Source: the words of the person in `../RECORD.md`. Use it to read each
document before it goes to the person. A head can read it; it is not a form.

1. **Answer the original question.** The body is what the reader must know: the patterns, what agrees with
   other stocks, why some moved and some did not. It is not a list of answers to follow-up questions.
2. **No status block at the top.** No "preliminary", no store number, no branch, no "of record" list in the
   text that the person reads. Put the status of the numbers in one line at the end, or in the sources file.
3. **An explanation, not "no cause found".** The person requires an explanation that was tested and that
   other data supports. If the cause is not in our data, the Head of Economics finds what the market says
   is the reason (the headlines, the consensus), tests it against our data, and says what the market implies
   for the time after, with our engines.
4. **Words as strong as the evidence.** "Consistent with its sector", not "proven". Do not write "proven"
   for one week of data.
5. **Leave out what does not help the question.** A refuted side idea does not go on the page.
6. **Tables.** A title ("Table 1: ...") and one line that says what to see. A note below a table has one or
   two lines at most.
7. **Valuation numbers as blocks.** A value is one block made of small blocks: show it as a table or as
   bullet points, not as numbers in a sentence.
8. **A title and a subtitle.** The title is a short name, not a sentence and not a question. The subtitle
   says what the document covers.
9. **A small intro at the top.** One to three lines of necessary background, after the title and the
   subtitle and before the answer.
10. Short, clear, ASD-STE100.
